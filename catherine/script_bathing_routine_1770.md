# My Bathing Routine, 1770 (Catherine the Great Vlog)

**Hook**

Dobroye utro, my little serfs.

After my morning routine, you wrote to me. Hundreds of you. One question. Does the Empress of all the Russias actually wash?

Impertinent. But yes. Today you see everything. Ice, fire, birch branches, a frozen river, and Turkish rose oil.

Turkish. While I am at war with the Turks. We will get to that.

It is Saturday. Banya day. Come.

**Set up**

Five o'clock. Still dark. In Petersburg in December, it is always still dark.

I light my own fire. I make my own coffee. Nobody else is allowed to drink it. It would kill them.

Maria Savvishna is already awake. Maria is always already awake. I suspect she does not sleep. I suspect she does not need to.

Out, Zakhar. Take the dog.

Not that dog. That dog stays.

This is Sir Tom Anderson. English greyhound. A gift from the doctor who gave me smallpox. He came with a better title than most of my generals.

He sleeps in my room, on a silk cushion, with his own blanket. He has a servant. The servant has a servant.

Sir Tom does not bathe. Sir Tom refuses. We respect each other's choices.

**Subject 1 — The face**

First, before anything, ice.

A block from the Neva. I rub it on my face. Every morning. Forty-one years old, and look.

Do not look too closely.

The French ladies paint their faces white. I freeze mine. Cheaper. Also, it wakes you up faster than any husband ever did.

I had one. He did not wake me up. He kept me up. With violins. And toy soldiers. In the bed.

He is gone now. We do not discuss it.

Then, teeth. Warm water, a little salt, a linen cloth. You rub. You rinse. You smile at no one, because it is five in the morning.

I do not eat much sugar. I see your comments about the English queen. I am not her. I have all my teeth. Mostly.

**Subject 2 — The banya**

Now, the real thing. Follow me. Quietly. They do not know you are watching.

In Paris, they splash water on a cloth and call it washing. Voltaire writes to me about philosophy. He does not write to me about soap. I think there is a reason.

In Russia, we have the banya. Every Russian has the banya. The peasant has one. I have one. Mine is nicer.

Behind that wall, a stove. Stones on top. Three men feed it since yesterday. Birch logs, half a forest.

Thank you, Ivan.

I do not know if he is Ivan. Everyone is Ivan. It is efficient.

The water comes from the Neva. Men carry it in barrels. Up the stairs. Through the galleries. Past my paintings. I bought those paintings to annoy the King of Prussia. Do not splash them.

You are wondering if I feel bad for the water carriers.

They are warm. I am warm. Everybody is warm. This is called enlightened government.

**Subject 3 — The steam**

Water on the stones. Hiss.

Then the heat comes for you like a Cossack.

The doctors say steam opens the body and lets the bad humours out. I do not argue with doctors. Two years ago, I let an English doctor give me smallpox. On purpose. You saw that video.

So, steam. Easy.

Maria, the venik.

This is a venik. Birch branches, tied, soaked in hot water. She beats me with it.

Yes. A servant beats the Empress. In any other room, this is treason. In the banya, this is love.

Harder, Maria.

Not that hard.

My Grand Duke Paul asked once if it hurts. I said ruling Russia hurts. This is a massage.

**Hook**

Now. The Turkish rose oil.

This summer, my fleet met the Turkish fleet at Chesme. We burned all of it. In one night. The whole Ottoman navy, gone.

Europe is still picking its jaw up off the floor.

And this little bottle? Attar of roses. From the Ottoman lands. Thousands of petals for one drop.

Is it treason to smell like the enemy?

I decide what treason is.

This is not treason. This is spoils of war.

I bought it before the war. Same thing.

**Set up**

Now the best part. The part the Prussian ambassador will not believe.

Out of the steam, and into the snow.

Yes. Snow.

Maria opens the little door. Outside, the courtyard. White. Minus twenty.

And I go out in my shift.

[gasps]

Every Russian does this. You heat yourself like bread, then you throw yourself in the cold. The heart jumps. The blood runs. You feel twenty-five.

For about four seconds.

Back in. Back in. Back in.

Then once more. Steam. Snow. Steam. Three times, for health. Four times, for showing off.

Maria refuses to do four. Maria is wise.

**Subject 1 — Hair**

My hair. Washed with warm water and rye bread kvass, to make it shine. The French use powder. I also use powder. Washing it out takes longer than winning a war.

Combed. Slowly.

Slower, Maria.

You only see it under powder at court. Underneath, it is brown. Mine. Do not tell anyone.

**Subject 2 — The linen**

My wet shift goes to the laundresses.

In winter, they wash in the palace. Then they rinse in the river. Through a hole in the ice.

Their hands are red. Their language is redder. I have learned many new words. I will not teach you them.

Then the linen hangs in the attic and freezes stiff. You could stand it in a corner. It could attend a council meeting. It would say more than some ministers.

**Subject 3 — The peasants**

And you, my little serfs? How do you wash?

Exactly like me.

Every Saturday, every village. The little wooden banya by the river. Steam, birch, snow.

This is the one thing in my empire where the peasant and the empress are equal.

Do not get ideas.

Last year, a Frenchman told me the Russians bathe more than anyone in Europe. He meant it as an insult. I took it as a compliment. I take everything as a compliment. It saves time.

Foreigners come here and write home that Russians run naked into the snow. Scandal. Barbarians. Then they try it once, and they never write again. Too busy.

**Subject 4 — Dressing**

Now, dried by the fire. Wrapped in linen, warmed on the stove.

A drop of rose oil, here. Turkish. Victorious.

Then a simple gown for the morning. Loose. No corset before ten. I made this law for myself. It is the most popular law I have ever written.

And then, to work. Letters to Voltaire. Letters to my generals. Letters to Grigory.

Grigory does not get to see the banya. Grigory gets to see the result.

**Outro / Conclusion**

So, that is how an empress gets clean.

Ice, coffee, fire, steam, birch, snow, kvass, a river with a hole in it, and Turkish roses.

Two hours. Every Saturday. Worth every log.

Now, tell me in the comments. Have you ever jumped in the snow after a banya? And if not, why not? Coward.

And before you go, my little serfs. Under this video there is a small button. Hype. Press it. Because I am the Empress, and because Maria was beaten by birch branches for this video.

No. I was. She did the beating. Same thing.

Subscribe. And wash behind your ears.

I have spies.
